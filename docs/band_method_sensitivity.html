Band Method Sensitivity

The same per-trial data, scored four defensible ways. Confidence bands are not a property of the data alone — they depend on which statistic you summarise with, whether datasets are pooled, and whether a metric is averaged per trial or recomputed over the pooled signal. This page shows how much each choice moves the answer, and where it changes a conclusion rather than just a number.

8 checkpoints2000 resamples synthetic · 4608 trials organic · 243 participants reconstruction R²
One choice decides whether the crank failure is visible at all.

Pooled across datasets with the analysis notebook's settings, the reproduced pretrained checkpoint is indistinguishable from the released one when summarised by the median trial — and clearly worse when summarised by the mean. The median is robust to exactly the catastrophic trials that constitute the failure, so it reports the typical trial faithfully and the tail not at all.

median · released vs rerun 0.9477 vs 0.9444gap +0.0033
mean · released vs rerun 0.8805 vs 0.8135gap +0.0669

Neither is wrong. They answer different questions, and only one of them can see a model that fails badly on a minority of trials.

Pooling with balance_datasets=True upsamples every dataset to the largest row count, so crank — five participants — is averaged in with whacamole's 176. Splitting per dataset is what made the crank discrepancy legible; it also makes the bands honest about how few clusters support them.

All variants recomputed from the same per-trial dumps, so differences are attributable purely to method. Pooled metrics are reconstructed exactly from per-trial sufficient statistics (n, Σy, Σy², SS_res), verified against direct computation. Percentile intervals, no bias correction, no multiple-comparison adjustment.
